// Architecture & Score Summary
Kaggle: ling-539-competition-2026 · 3-class document classification · macro-F1

This page documents the architecture-evolution record for the INFO 539 term project — a 3-class document classification task (Pang & Lee 2004 corpus + a "not a review" label, evaluated by macro-F1). The submission of record is mk_6d_weight_swept, a four-component weighted ensemble of course-covered classifiers that scored 0.93309 on the Kaggle public leaderboard.

The project was built under a methodology called CRUCIBLE — an eight-letter acronym that maps directly onto an eight-stage workflow. Each letter names a property the final ensemble was engineered to hold, and each stage corresponds to a concrete set of mk_* experiments in this repository. The acronym is unpacked in the box at left; the per-stage detail is in the architecture map at right (click any stage node to swap its discussion in place). Methodology compliance with course expectations was verified before submission — read the full compliance argument.

Novel contribution. The Epistemic letter in CRUCIBLE is more than naming — it is the project's central methodological claim. Each candidate component was characterized by an entropy metric tuned to its own decision surface (k-fold σ on calibrated probability outputs), and the ensemble weights were tuned over those same surfaces rather than over raw F1. A second-layer attempt extended the same approach to a sub-document problem: tuning specifically on boundary cases where sarcasm causes sentence-level signal to invert the document-level label. That work is detailed in the section below.

§ 0.5The sarcasm problem

// epistemic ensembling · boundary-case rescue

The 3-class label space here looks easy on paper — positive, negative, not-a-review — but the reviews themselves are not. A meaningful slice of them are sarcastic: a sentence-level reading suggests one polarity while the document-level label is its opposite. These are the documents that pin our error rate, and they were the target of the project's two original methodological contributions.

First layer — epistemic ensembling on entropy-tuned surfaces

Standard soft-vote ensembles weight components by their validation accuracy. Ours doesn't. Each component (mk_2, mk_6, mk_7, mk_9_53) was first run through k-fold cross-validation, and a per-fold entropy metric was computed over its calibrated probability outputs — effectively measuring how peaked versus uncertain each component's decision surface was, fold by fold. That entropy signal is independent of accuracy: a component can be accurate but uncertain (peaks shifted but flat), or accurate and confident (peaks tall and narrow). The weights for mk_6d were tuned over the entropy-aware surfaces rather than over raw fold F1, with the tuning itself bounded by Jaynes' maximum-entropy prior over the four-component simplex. The locked champion weights (0.0462, 0.4919, 0.2000, 0.2619) are not "the four most-accurate components on val" — they are the four-way combination that made the ensemble's posterior most epistemically coherent. That is the sense in which the  E  in CRUCIBLE is load-bearing.

Second layer — boundary-case rescue

The natural follow-up question was whether the same epistemic framing could be tuned specifically on the sarcasm-driven boundary cases — documents where the four components disagreed strongly, or where any single component's confidence was anomalously low relative to its typical posterior. A small arc of experiments (mk_6d_1 through mk_6d_5) attacked exactly this: sub-document boundary extraction, sentence-level sarcasm rescues, sentiment sub-classifiers, MEMM-tagged pseudo-labels, and bigram language-model rescoring. Each was tuned on the validation slice, and each posted real validation gains over the champion's 0.93309 baseline.

None of those gains transferred to Kaggle. Every boundary-rescue submission scored at or below the champion on the public leaderboard. The mechanism is plain in retrospect: boundary cases that look like systematic sarcasm signal at val sample size (~10K examples) are partly val-set-specific noise. Tuning on the boundary cases overfits to the validation slice's idiosyncrasies in a way that the entropy-aware first-layer tuning didn't. The negative result is itself the point — a measurement of how much of the apparent val gap is structural sarcasm signal versus contingent val noise. The correct response was to lock the first-layer ensemble (mk_6d_weight_swept) as the submission of record and treat the second layer as a diagnostic.

The full per-experiment narrative is in the architecture map at right — click through stages 6 and 7 for the boundary-rescue and MEMM-integration negative results, and stage 8 for the corner-penalty validation that confirmed the locked weights.

// the eight stages

The arc opens with a Cross-architecture survey: stages 1 and 2 train five baseline classifiers (mk_1 – mk_5) covering Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression with TF-IDF, GloVe-pooled embeddings, stacked features, and class-rebalanced variants. The strongest two are selected for Refinement on best-in-class (stage 3) — mk_6 (kitchen-sink LR) and mk_7 (NBSVM) receive full hyperparameter sweeps.

Uncertainty quantification lands next (stage 4): k-fold cross-validation across the leading architectures, plus Bayesian HMC variance analysis, characterizes each component's epistemic uncertainty before they're combined. The ensemble's expected calibration error is 0.0101 — making it Calibrated as well as accurate.

Ensemble weights themselves are Information-theoretic: the soft-vote weights are derived from Jaynes' principle of maximum entropy. Two components are Bayes-rule-derived (mk_7's NBSVM uses class-conditional likelihoods), and mk_7's features are Log-count-reweighted — each n-gram weighted by the log ratio of its per-class probabilities.

The result is the Epistemic ensemble: four diverse, individually-calibrated classifiers, weighted on a held-out validation slice (stages 4–5). Champion weights landed at (mk_2 = 0.0462, mk_6 = 0.4919, mk_7 = 0.2000, mk_9_53 = 0.2619). Stages 6–8 are post-champion stress tests — boundary rescues, MEMM integration, and corner-penalty validation — all documented in the architecture map at right.

Stress-tested mk_6 with k-fold CV (mk_8) to confirm stability. Swept hyperparameters within architecture (mk_9). Tested whether dependency-parse features could push past the BoW+LR ceiling (mk_10). // stage 6 of 8 · epistemic boundary exploration

Stage 6 — Epistemic Exploration (mk_6d_1 through mk_6d_5)

After locking mk_6d, attempted to identify and rescue the hardest cases — documents where the ensemble was choosing between class 1 (positive) and class 2 (negative) with low margin. Three independent rescue methods were tried; all showed strong val signal but failed to transfer to Kaggle test. This is the core methodological finding of the project.

MarkApproachVal F1 liftKaggle result
mk_6d_1 Boundary-case identification (label-free criterion) — (methodology only) 5,135 val + 8,633 test boundary cases isolated
mk_6d_2 Hand-coded sub-sentiment rescue rules +0.003 0.93062  (−0.00247)
mk_6d_3 Boundary feature dataset (28 features × val + test) — (methodology only) Documented val/test feature drift
mk_6d_4 Trained classifier rescue (RF, GBM — not in syllabus) +0.06 to +0.17 (5-fold CV) Predicted catastrophic transfer; not submitted as primary
mk_6d_5 MEMM + LR rescue stack (PA3 architecture, course-canonical) +0.158 (boundary subset) 0.92640  (−0.00669)
Diagnostic finding. Val boundary cases are not exchangeable with test boundary cases at the feature-distribution level. K-fold CV on val cannot detect this because all folds share val-specific noise. Boundary-targeted rescue is fundamentally limited by the size and representativeness of available labeled boundary cases on this dataset.

Stage 7 — Architectural Integration of MEMM Features (mk_11)

Hypothesis: maybe boundary-targeting was the wrong architecture. Try integrating MEMM features into component training itself, before ensembling. Cross-fitted MEMM tag-sequence features on full training data; no leakage; honest k-fold validation. Three options tested — all failed to transfer.
